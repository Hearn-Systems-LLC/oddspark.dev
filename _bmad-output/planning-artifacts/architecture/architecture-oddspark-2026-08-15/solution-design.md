# Oddspark Opportunity Brief Pipeline — Solution Design

*The human-readable companion to `ARCHITECTURE-SPINE.md`. The spine is the contract; this explains it. Where they disagree, the spine wins.*

## What we're building and why

Oddspark today composes sparks from four independent random word lists (who / why / what / sting), seeded by drand randomness and solar-flare data. The results can be contrived: they can recommend something a business already has, assume the wrong channel, or pitch work Hearn Systems wouldn't deliver. The Opportunity Brief pipeline replaces how a candidate idea is produced and vetted. The product shape remains one button, one optional domain field, the existing seed feeds and cache identities, and the existing visual shell. The governing UX record's closed D1, D1a, D2, D2a, and D3–D24 rows deliberately change only the shell's representation, progressive-enhancement, accessibility, state-copy, and result-card seams authorized by AD-6/AD-12; they are not general redesign authority.

The new core idea: **a spark is no longer drawn from lists; it is generated as a complete plan, checked locally, then judged against nine coherence gates before anyone sees it.** Failing Candidates are never shown. The strike starts only complete generation-to-judge pairs that fit its six-call ledger; when evidence uses no model call it can try three Candidates, and when evidence uses one it can try two. Exhaustion returns a curated, pre-vetted "house Brief" instead of a near-miss.

## The pipeline in one picture

```
button press
   │
   ▼
Strike orchestrator ──────────────── owns the six-call ledger and full deadline
   │
   ├─► 1 · Evidence     domain? scan the public site → evidence bundle
   │                    no domain? local priors (Port Huron, season, situation)
   │
   ├─► 2 · Generate     one candidate Brief, seeded by drand + solar window
   │
   ├─► 3 · Gate         local contract/privacy checks, then one semantic judge
   │         │ fail → orchestrator retries (or serves house Brief when exhausted)
   │         ▼ pass
   ├─► 4 · Commit       first pass wins the window; everyone else gets the same artifact
   │
   └─► 5 · Render       HTML / plain text / JSON, all from one typed Brief schema
```

## The decisions that matter

**The judge is not the generator, and the provider wire is not the internal contract.** The immutable `AttemptContext` carries the exact Candidate, closed Evidence Bundle, closed grounding report, rubric version, and a canonical hash of the Candidate. Local schema, grounding, PII-policy, and number-provenance checks reject first without a judge call. A surviving Candidate goes once to a role-qualified judge with that reference. The provider returns an outer `JudgeResult {candidate_ref, verdict}`; exact reference equality is required before its inner `{pass,gates[9],tone,claims}` verdict can map into Oddspark. The adapter cannot synthesize the reference, invent reasons, coerce types, fill omissions, or reinterpret drift. Binding proves which Candidate came back, not that the judge evaluated it well, so semantic calibration remains separate.

**The receipt survives — honestly.** The site's founding joke is "randomness with a receipt": same window, same spark. LLMs aren't deterministic, but we never needed them to be. The *first* gate-passing Brief in a window is stored in an authoritative coordinator receipt; KV pins are fast read projections, not the consistency authority. A missing or stale pin checks the coordinator before generation. Every later visitor in that window therefore gets the identical versioned artifact. We claim reproducibility of the committed artifact, not of the model. No reproducibility promise goes into page copy until this is verified in production.

**Grounding and privacy have one owner.** A submitted public page may contain PII only in the ephemeral scan buffer needed to detect it; detected PII is discarded before evidence persistence or model input. Every business-specific claim and grounded number must trace to the sanitized evidence, and the single charming "breadcrumb" must be an exact substring of canonicalized scan text — checked once, at the schema stage. Mechanically decidable PII stays local; Story 1.7's non-model personal-name policy returns `pass`, `fail`, or `unknown`, and both non-pass states reject. The judge consumes that report; it cannot override or re-verify it against raw HTML. A grounding/privacy failure means retry, never a quiet downgrade.

**Privacy is structural, not a promise.** Only the submitted public site is scanned (robots.txt respected); no reviews, no off-site research, no PII in breadcrumbs. Website-grounded Briefs get no public permalink — a critique of someone's business never becomes a shareable URL. Analytics are server-side aggregate served-outcome counters only: an authoritative render increments `briefs_served`, house renders are its `house_briefs_served` subset, and accepted invitation actions increment `invitation_acted`; 400/404/502 responses enter none of them. Invitation engagement is an approximate event rate, not deduplicated per visitor/render, so repeat actions can skew it and it is never labeled as a true percentage. Reports disclose receiver-activation coverage because plain-link fallback actions are intentionally uncounted. Public launch additionally requires `house_briefs_served / briefs_served` to remain strictly below 10% over at least 100 authoritative production serve events; slow organic accumulation delays the gate and never authorizes manufactured traffic. Same-interval availability evidence retains aggregate response-class totals and coverage/query hashes only—never raw logs, IPs, URLs, submitted domains, or artifact ids. The existing rate limiting is a declared abuse carve-out, nothing more.

**The schema is the contract.** A Brief is one typed JSON object with the eight contract elements — title, plan, why-it-fits, what-gets-better, before/after, change level, what-stays-the-same, invitation. Numbers are structurally confined to the (always preliminary) change-level estimate and a locally provenance-verified `grounded_numbers` list. Renderers can't drift because they never see raw model text.

**One strike has two representations.** `/api/spark` has one validation, generation, authoritative commit, and failure contract. Explicit `Accept: application/json` wins; otherwise an HTML-accepting or browser-form request receives shell HTML, and remaining requests preserve JSON behavior. Enhanced/API success returns the committed artifact as JSON. A native local-scope success redirects with `303` to its server-rendered `/s/:id`; the redirect counts nothing, and the followed GET renders and counts once. A native domain-scoped success—including a local-mode downgrade—returns direct `200` home-shell HTML from `/api/spark` because it has no permalink; the browser URL remains `/api/spark`, with no redirect or history mutation, and refresh may re-submit through the same authoritative claim path. The enhanced domain path remains at its current page URL because it performs no history mutation. The same `400` and `502` meanings remain, rendered as JSON or inside the shell according to the request; unknown, expired, unsupported, and domain-scoped permalinks return the shared `404` shell. Local refresh re-reads the permalink. Enhanced in-place outcomes follow the UX focus rules, while a fresh full-document response sets no scripted focus. Every response emits the selected `Content-Type`; `Vary` retains `Origin` and adds `Accept` and `Content-Type`. Dynamic responses are `no-store`; COORD remains authority, KV remains a projection, and neither HTTP caching nor `history.replaceState` creates artifact or metric authority. `briefs_served` increments at the JSON `200`, the domain-scope HTML `200`, or the followed local permalink GET—never at the local `303`; house-Brief counting follows the same delivery points.

**Cost and latency are capped.** The route-entry deadline covers evidence through authoritative commit. Evidence freezes its call count first; each Candidate attempt reserves a complete generation–judge pair, consuming each slot immediately before its call and releasing an unused judge reservation after local rejection. Invoked errors and timeouts stay consumed. Each role has its own qualified selector. The six-call ledger is the hard cap; the existing Workers AI neuron meter remains best-effort/fail-open and cannot create extra capacity. Another provider needs an approved cost-control and security/operational decision. Over budget, unqualified, unavailable, uncertain at commit, or out of time → authoritative committed/house-Brief path.

**Qualification is staged and hash-bound.** Story 1.2 first freezes the reviewed Wrangler, compatibility-date, bindings, and runtime baseline. Story 1.4 then proves provider-wire shape, canonical mapping, candidate-reference binding, and isolated judge latency/cost for each primary/fallback independently. Story 1.11 separately proves typed generation output. Story 2.3 independently proves model-assisted Website-Grounded Evidence output; that call makes `E=1`, leaving two complete Candidate pairs. Story 1.18 proves semantics against Justin's versioned rubric and golden/anti-golden corpus. Story 1.19 proves the local full request, and Story 2.8 verifies the domain full request including Evidence. Each stage emits an immutable manifest; a later runtime-bound change invalidates affected evidence and every dependent stage. Production consumes one closed atomic activation value with the shared Evidence-independent generation, judge, and semantic refs represented once and only mode-specific Evidence/full-request refs nested by mode. Runtime derives its hash and rejects parallel or individually supplied activation state. Runtime recomputes each configured role identity and disables a missing, stale, failed, or mismatched role. Rates never pool and fallbacks never replace failed primary trials.

**Release progression is a dependency graph with one deliberate convergence.** Story 1.2's toolchain/runtime baseline precedes live qualification. Story 1.13 builds the orchestrator offline; Story 1.14 establishes the compatibility reader and authoritative coordinator path before the new writer; Stories 1.18 and 1.19 then supply semantic and local full-request evidence. Story 1.20 defines the single activation value. Story 1.23 assembles the one canonical runtime-neutral pipeline behind the Worker boundary without deployment; Story 1.24 deploys the compatibility reader; Story 1.25 deploys the assembled compatible writer in an inactive safe posture without an active manifest; and Story 1.26, under separate activation authority, publishes the local-only value atomically and last. From there, Story 3.1 → 3.2 → 3.3 produces local proof and the first owner review, while Stories 2.1–2.10 independently complete and activate the domain path. Those branches may finish in either order, and neither grants authority to the other. Story 3.4 waits for both Story 3.3 and Story 2.10 before domain production proof; Story 3.5 is the separately approved quiet-production operational checkpoint; and Story 3.6 separately governs receipt-claim approval and activation. Rollback keeps a compatible reader and removes or replaces the whole manifest when it no longer matches deployed code. A malformed manifest disables model roles, claim copy, and reference handoff while preserving the authoritative committed/approved-house route when safe. Receipt/reproducibility copy additionally requires its exact claim-proof ref, otherwise the honest non-claiming copy remains. The quiet-production report pairs authoritative COORD deltas with complete same-interval platform HTTP outcomes; unexplained 5xx or incomplete coverage blocks PASS without entering the house-rate arithmetic. One deterministic on-demand decision view makes every passed, blocked, stale, or unapproved gate visible without creating new state, while implementation, deployment, activation, production proof, observation, promotion, claim activation, and destructive retirement retain separate approvals.

## What changes in the code (and what doesn't)

Stays: the route inventory except the already authorized `/api/cheer`, KV key and cache-authority scheme, seed feeds, scan budgets, abuse limits, one-button/optional-domain product shape, visual identity, and the macro page grid and breakpoints outside the authorized deltas. Changes are limited to UX D1, D1a, D2, D2a, and D3–D24: the native strike form, content negotiation, server-rendered eligible local permalinks, accessibility semantics, governed state copy, and eight-element card mapping. SparkCoordinator gains the narrow authoritative Brief receipt/read/commit behavior and atomic aggregate metrics; NeuronMeter keeps its existing best-effort behavior.

Goes: the four axis word-lists and their string-concatenation fallback.

New banner sections in the single-file worker: evidence assembly (both modes), immutable attempt/grounding contracts, generate, composite Gate + role-qualified judge adapter, qualification manifests/activation, house Briefs, Brief schema, and the strike orchestrator. The existing router changes only at the closed AD-12 representation seam for `POST /api/spark` and server-rendered eligible local `GET /s/:id`, plus one new endpoint (`POST /api/cheer`) that counts accepted invitation actions and redirects to the allowlisted Hearn contact form with only an opaque spark id when a matching receiver contract is active. Without it, the renderer uses the plain fixed Hearn contact link and records no invitation event. The receiving contact form integration is separately authorized cross-repository work. Domain request scope remains domain-scoped even after a local-mode downgrade; effective mode controls rendering. `/s/:id` refuses domain artifacts, and `/api/spark/:id` serves them only during the Justin-approved one-hour domain-result lifetime implemented by Story 2.6.

## Before launch — the human deliverables

The architecture deliberately defers three content decisions to Justin, all pre-launch:

1. **The voice rubric + at least 3 golden Briefs per mode** (from the PRD). The judge's tone checks and the gate-9 "surprising but believable" threshold are calibrated against these. Without them the gates run on my defaults, not yours.
2. **The house Brief catalog** — the curated seasonal fallbacks, written alongside the goldens.
3. **The `/how` page rewrite** — it still documents the axis-collision flow.
