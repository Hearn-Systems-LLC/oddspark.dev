- (direction by user) Use First Principles Thinking, then Reverse Brainstorming, then one invented technique, then one coach-selected technique.
- (technique by coach) Invented Coherence Casting: begin with a familiar human situation, cast one fitting capability and one credible payoff, then reject any concept that cannot be retold naturally to a friend.
- (decision by coach) Selected Job to Be Done as the fourth technique to uncover what a normie is hiring Oddspark to do beneath the idea-generator interface.
- (technique by coach) started First Principles Thinking
- (idea by coach) Treat the submitted domain as evidence about the user's world, not as a random creative seed; delight should come from feeling accurately recognized before being surprised.
- (insight by user) The experience succeeds when it produces a novel spark that makes someone consider doing something differently from how they do it today.
- (idea by coach) Anchor every generated spark in a recognizable current behavior, then introduce one surprising but imaginable change to that behavior instead of generating a detached product concept.
- (insight by user) The best target is a repetitive or habitual process that could be more efficient, especially one the user resists changing because it already just works.
- (idea by coach) Frame sparks as preserving the trusted ritual while quietly removing its most annoying step: keep doing X, but never do Y again.
- (idea by user) Use known recurring annoyances associated with the business vertical as clues to a worthwhile habitual process.
- (idea by user) Broken or weak elements on the submitted website could reveal processes or customer experiences worth changing.
- (idea by user) Less-than-perfect Google reviews could reveal normalized annoyances, though this evidence is outside the domain itself.
- (insight by coach) The domain can function as an identity key that opens a small evidence bundle—site signals, vertical priors, and public customer friction—rather than being the literal boundary of what Oddspark may understand.
- (decision by user) The experience should feel like playful riffing while investigating deeply enough to avoid repeatedly producing the most obvious idea.
- (idea by coach) Keep research backstage and surface only one charming breadcrumb that proves the riff belongs to this business; the experience feels like a perceptive improv partner rather than an audit.
- (decision by user) Personalization must not use personally identifiable information, session or cookie data, or any user data beyond what the submitted business website publicly reveals.
- (insight by coach) Google reviews and broader business-footprint investigation are outside the chosen trust boundary; novelty must come from deeper interpretation of the public website plus general vertical knowledge, not broader personal or behavioral surveillance.
- (idea by user) Treat missing website capabilities as evidence of a habitual process or customer interaction that may be ready for improvement.
- (idea by user) Use technical website behavior as evidence for possible improvements.
- (idea by user) Messy or complex navigation can reveal operational or customer-experience friction worth riffing on.
- (idea by user) Broken images or links are fair website-specific signals for generating a spark.
- (idea by user) Out-of-date website content can reveal stale processes or neglected customer touchpoints.
- (technique by coach) started Reverse Brainstorming
- (idea by coach) To guarantee contrived output, select independent random axes without compatibility rules, then force the model to rationalize their collision after the fact.
- (idea by user) To make the product useless, recommend implementing a capability the website already has, such as a scheduling app when Calendly or an equivalent is already present.
- (insight by coach) Inverse: build a detected-capabilities inventory and reject any candidate whose core job is already served; existing tools should become constraints or extension points, not duplicate recommendations.
- (idea by user) To make the output irrelevant, assume the wrong communication channel—for example, design around phone calls for an email-only business or tell customers to call a business that monitors only Discord.
- (insight by coach) Inverse: extract a channel map from links, calls to action, forms, contact text, and integrations; generated ideas may use only observed channels unless explicitly framed as introducing a new one for a compelling reason.
- (idea by user) To mismatch scale downward, recommend a spreadsheet with elaborate macros as the CRM for a multimillion-dollar company.
- (idea by user) To mismatch scale upward, recommend a huge database implementation for a one-person operation.
- (insight by coach) Inverse: apply a proportionality gate so solution weight matches observable organizational scale, operational complexity, and likely maintenance capacity.
- (idea by user) To make the experience annoying, identify a problem that has no clear solution and point it out merely for the sake of finding fault.
- (insight by coach) Inverse: every surfaced friction must unlock an imaginable intervention; Oddspark is a spark generator, not a website critic, so suppress observations that cannot lead somewhere constructive.
- (decision by user) Every suggestion must concern work Hearn Systems could credibly help implement; the experience should get the visitor thinking about working with Hearn Systems, not offer unrelated operational advice such as buying heavy machinery.
- (insight by coach) Inverse: constrain solution generation to Hearn Systems' credible delivery envelope—custom software, AI automation, integrations, data workflows, and adjacent digital systems—without turning the playful result into an overt sales pitch.
- (technique by coach) started Coherence Casting
- (insight by coach) Port Huron Family Dentists publicly emphasizes late Monday hours for busy weeks, phone-first scheduling, new-patient acceptance, online forms, comfort, and an in-house membership plan.
- (idea by coach) Monday Night Matchmaker: preserve phone-first scheduling while offering a lightweight opt-in that fills short-notice late-Monday openings by notifying suitable interested families; the spark riffs on the site's distinctive late-hours promise rather than proposing generic online scheduling.
- (decision by user) Present each spark as a confident plan with its rationale and expected improvement, not as a hypothetical what-if question.
- (decision by user) Describe the required level of technical and process change in owner-understandable terms so the idea does not feel overwhelming merely because the owner cannot assess the technology.
- (idea by coach) Give every result a stable owner-facing structure: The Plan, Why It Fits, What Gets Better, and Change Level measured by disruption to the owner's routine rather than implementation jargon.
- (decision by user) Express change level with an estimated implementation-time range and the number of existing workflow steps that would change.
- (idea by coach) Show adoption impact concretely, such as Estimated setup: 2–4 weeks; workflow impact: one existing step changes and one repetitive step disappears, with estimates clearly presented as preliminary.
- (decision by user) Each spark should name the current workflow steps that remain untouched so the owner can see what they will not need to relearn.
- (idea by coach) Add a What Stays the Same section to every result, explicitly protecting familiar tools, decision authority, and unchanged workflow steps.
- (insight by user) Family Dentists' Google Business Profile advertises Online Booking and links to an appointment flow the office does not actually support; the owners do not know how to remove it.
- (idea by coach) Booking Promise Fix: reconcile the Google Business Profile booking promise with the real office workflow by removing the unsupported path or replacing it with a supported request-and-confirm flow; preserve the existing scheduling authority.
- (idea by coach) Add an optional owner-supplied 'one weird thing we should know' field so Oddspark can use volunteered operational context while remaining website-only and privacy-safe by default.
- (decision by user) Preserve the current one-button interaction; a domain name may remain the only optional input, and no additional input fields should be added now.
- (insight by coach) The domain should alter the generator's evidence source, not add interface complexity: no domain yields a coherent general spark; a domain yields a website-grounded spark through the same button.
- (idea by user) Seed no-domain generation with software, AI, automation, and online opportunities that Hearn Systems can deliver.
- (idea by user) Make no-domain sparks specific to small businesses in Port Huron and the Blue Water Area.
- (idea by user) Pass current date and time into generation so sparks can use relevant seasonal business context.
- (insight by coach) No-domain mode can remain privacy-safe and relatable by combining fixed local context, current seasonality, compatible business situations, and Hearn-deliverable capability bundles instead of independent random axes.
- (technique by coach) started Job to Be Done
- (insight by user) The visitor has lived with a longstanding problem since starting the work, suspects a smarter solution exists, but has never made time to design it; Oddspark unexpectedly delivers that solution.
- (insight by coach) The core job is compressed problem-solving: recognize a normalized frustration, do the thinking the owner has deferred, and return a concrete plan that makes the smart solution feel approachable.
- (decision by user) Demonstrate understanding by contrasting the outcome of continuing without the solution against the outcome after adopting it.
- (idea by coach) Add a concise Before / After contrast to each spark, describing the recurring cost of the current routine and the changed business or human outcome once the plan exists.
- (decision by user) Keep the Before / After outcome contrast qualitative unless the submitted website provides real numeric evidence.
- (insight by coach) Avoid fabricated ROI and false precision; use truthful qualitative consequences by default and only reuse numbers grounded in the public site.
- (decision by user) The desired next action is for the visitor to contact Hearn Systems and ask how to get the proposed solution implemented.
- (idea by coach) End each result with an implementation-oriented invitation tied to the exact spark, so contacting Hearn Systems feels like continuing the plan rather than entering a generic sales funnel.
- (insight by user) The primary hesitation after a resonant spark is the assumption that implementation will be too expensive.
- (idea by coach) Reduce cost anxiety by showing a smallest useful version and inviting a bounded feasibility conversation, without inventing a price before discovery.
- (direction by user) Hearn Systems is problem-solving-led, not sales-led: identify the real problem, design a solution that ideally requires nothing from the user, minimize process change and friction, never push unwanted work, and make the conversation explicitly no-pressure and no-obligation.
- (idea by coach) CTA: Talk through the problem, no pitch attached. Hearn Systems will help test whether the idea fits, find the lowest-friction version, and say so if it is not worth changing.
- (decision by coach) Affinity clustering surfaces one proposed direction: replace independent random axes with a coherence-gated Opportunity Brief pipeline that preserves the one-button interface, uses either website evidence or local seasonal priors, and outputs a confident low-friction implementation plan with an honest Hearn Systems invitation.
- (decision by user) Final governing principle: do not break what already works; preserve every helpful part of the existing process.
- (insight by coach) Final synthesis: Oddspark should identify the smallest worthwhile change around a trusted process, using novelty to remove normalized friction rather than replace helpful behavior; this principle unifies evidence selection, coherence gates, adoption framing, and Hearn Systems' no-pressure implementation posture.
