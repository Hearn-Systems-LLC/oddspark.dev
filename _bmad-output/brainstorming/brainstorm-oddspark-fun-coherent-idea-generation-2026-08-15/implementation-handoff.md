# Oddspark Opportunity Brief — Implementation Handoff

## Chosen product direction

Replace independent random-axis generation with a **coherence-gated Opportunity Brief pipeline**. Keep the current one-button interaction and optional domain input. With a domain, generate a website-grounded spark; without one, generate a coherent local and seasonally relevant spark. In both modes, return a confident, low-friction plan that Hearn Systems could credibly implement.

**Governing principle:** Do not break what already works; preserve every helpful part of the existing process. Find the smallest worthwhile change around a trusted routine and remove normalized friction instead of replacing helpful behavior.

## Current → target behavior

- **Current failure mode:** independent random axes can collide into contrived ideas, duplicate capabilities already present, assume the wrong channel, mismatch solution scale, criticize without a constructive intervention, or recommend work outside Hearn Systems' delivery envelope.
- **Target behavior:** recognize a familiar habitual process, use compatible evidence and context to uncover a longstanding annoyance, propose one surprising but imaginable improvement, and explain how the change protects the owner's existing tools, authority, and routines.

## Generation modes

1. **Website-grounded mode (domain supplied):** treat the public business website as an identity key and evidence source. Inspect its stated capabilities, missing capabilities, CTAs, forms, links, contact channels, integrations, navigation complexity, technical behavior, broken images or links, and stale content. Combine those signals with general vertical knowledge. Surface only one charming breadcrumb that shows why the spark belongs to this business; do not present an audit.
2. **Coherent local mode (no domain):** combine fixed Port Huron / Blue Water Area small-business context, current date/time and seasonality, a compatible business situation, and a Hearn-deliverable capability bundle. Seed around software, AI, automation, integrations, data workflows, online opportunities, and adjacent digital systems. Do not select independent random axes and rationalize their collision afterward.

## Evidence and privacy boundary

- By default, use only what the submitted public business website reveals, plus general vertical knowledge and current date/time.
- Do not use personally identifiable information, cookies, sessions, or other user data.
- Do not investigate Google reviews or the broader business footprint.
- Do not add another input field; the domain remains the only optional input.
- Numeric claims may appear only when grounded in real numbers on the public site. Otherwise, describe effects qualitatively and avoid fabricated ROI or false precision.

## Coherence gates

Reject a candidate unless all are true:

1. **Recognizable routine:** it begins from an observable or credible current behavior and a normalized recurring annoyance.
2. **Constructive intervention:** every friction leads to an imaginable solution; no fault-finding for its own sake.
3. **Capability inventory:** the core job is not already served by the site's existing tools. Existing tools are constraints or extension points, not targets for duplication.
4. **Channel fit:** the plan uses observed communication channels, unless it clearly and compellingly introduces a new one.
5. **Proportionality:** solution weight matches observable organizational scale, operational complexity, and likely maintenance capacity.
6. **Delivery fit:** Hearn Systems could credibly implement it through custom software, AI automation, integrations, data workflows, or adjacent digital systems.
7. **Preservation:** the plan retains every helpful tool, workflow step, and decision right it does not need to change.
8. **Natural retelling:** a person could explain the situation, fitting capability, and payoff naturally to a friend.
9. **Novel but imaginable:** it goes beyond the most obvious idea while remaining concrete and approachable.

## Exact result-card contract

Each result must be a confident plan, not a hypothetical question, and contain these elements:

1. **Spark title** — a memorable name for the specific plan.
2. **The Plan** — the smallest useful version of the intervention in owner-understandable terms.
3. **Why It Fits** — the evidence or context behind the recommendation, including one restrained, charming website breadcrumb in domain mode.
4. **What Gets Better** — the expected human or business improvement without unsupported numeric claims.
5. **Before / After** — a concise qualitative contrast between continuing the current routine and adopting the plan; use numbers only when the public site supplies them.
6. **Change Level** — a clearly preliminary estimated implementation-time range plus the number of existing workflow steps that change and, where applicable, repetitive steps that disappear.
7. **What Stays the Same** — explicitly name preserved tools, decision authority, and untouched workflow steps.
8. **Implementation invitation** — tie the invitation to this exact spark and the smallest useful version; offer a bounded feasibility conversation with no price invented before discovery.

**CTA posture:** “Talk through the problem, no pitch attached.” Help test whether the idea fits, find the lowest-friction version, and say so if it is not worth changing. The desired action is to ask Hearn Systems how the proposed solution could be implemented, but the experience must remain problem-solving-led, no-pressure, and no-obligation.

## Prioritized implementation tasks

### Phase 1 — Generation contract and gates

1. Define the two-mode input contract while preserving the single button and optional domain.
2. Define the candidate representation needed to evaluate routine, friction, capability, channel, proportionality, delivery fit, and preservation.
3. Implement the coherence gates and reject candidates that fail any gate.
4. Implement the ordered result-card contract, including preliminary change estimates and explicit preservation statements.
5. Constrain claims to qualitative outcomes unless the source website provides numeric evidence.

### Phase 2 — Evidence assembly

6. For domain mode, assemble the website-only evidence bundle and detected-capabilities/channel inventory.
7. For no-domain mode, assemble compatible local, seasonal, business-situation, and Hearn-deliverable priors using current date/time.
8. Ensure research remains backstage and only one business-specific breadcrumb appears in the result.

### Phase 3 — Adoption and conversion posture

9. Generate the smallest useful version and describe technical/process change in owner language.
10. Add the exact-spark, no-pressure implementation invitation and avoid pricing before discovery.
11. Validate results against representative contradictions: existing scheduler, channel mismatch, undersized/oversized solution, unactionable criticism, and non-digital advice.

## Acceptance criteria

- The interface still has one button and no new fields; a domain remains optional.
- The same action produces a coherent general/local spark without a domain and a website-grounded spark with one.
- Every displayed result passes all coherence gates and lies within Hearn Systems' credible delivery envelope.
- A result never recommends duplicating a detected capability or casually replacing a helpful existing process.
- Website mode stays within the public-site evidence boundary; neither mode uses PII, cookies, sessions, Google reviews, or broader footprint research.
- Every result follows the result-card contract, names what remains unchanged, and expresses change level as a preliminary time range plus workflow-step impact.
- Before / After is qualitative unless the website supplies the numbers used.
- The CTA is specific to the spark, no-pressure, no-obligation, and oriented toward testing fit and finding the lowest-friction implementation.
- A result can be retold naturally and offers a novel but believable change to a recognizable routine.

## Non-goals

- Adding user-input fields or increasing interaction complexity.
- Conducting a website audit or surfacing faults without solutions.
- Using reviews, off-site business-footprint research, personal data, or behavioral surveillance.
- Producing generic sales copy, unsupported ROI, false precision, or pre-discovery pricing.
- Recommending unrelated operational purchases or work Hearn Systems could not credibly deliver.
- Replacing functioning tools or processes merely to make an idea appear transformative.

## Risks and unknowns

- Website evidence may be incomplete, stale, contradictory, or insufficient to infer actual operations.
- Observable scale and maintenance capacity are estimates; proportionality judgments may be uncertain.
- Current website capabilities can be present but poorly exposed, creating duplication risk.
- Time ranges and workflow-step counts are necessarily preliminary until discovery.
- The threshold between a pleasingly surprising idea and an implausible one needs evaluation.
- The evidence needed to consistently avoid obvious ideas while remaining website-only is not yet proven.
